# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary audience is product and engineering leaders, hiring managers, founders, collaborators, and peers evaluating Marco Trevisani as a lead frontend engineer. They arrive quickly, often from search, social profiles, or a shared link, and need a fast read on credibility, taste, technical range, and where to go next.

Secondary users include people following Marco's creative projects, music identity, and Trevisoft side projects. They should feel that the engineering persona and creative persona belong to the same person.

## Product Purpose

This is a personal one-page site for marcotrevisani.com. Its job is to make Marco memorable as a Bologna-based lead frontend engineer who ships fast, polished React and Next.js product experiences, strong frontend architecture, and design systems that hold up under real product pressure.

Success looks like immediate recognition, clear professional positioning, confident links to Trevisoft and LinkedIn, and enough personality that the site does not feel like a generic resume page.

## Brand Personality

Expert, electric, product-minded.

The brand should feel like a technically serious frontend lead with a creative lab in the next room: precise enough to trust, kinetic enough to remember, and human enough that the social and audio signals feel intentional rather than decorative.

## Anti-references

- Generic corporate portfolio.
- Bland SaaS hero-metric template.
- Overproduced neon or crypto aesthetic.
- Static resume page that hides the human signal.
- Dense personal site that makes visitors hunt for the primary contact path.

## Design Principles

- Lead with identity. The name, role, portrait, and creative signal should be first-viewport facts, not supporting decoration.
- Make craft visible. Interactions, motion, and micro-details should quietly prove frontend taste.
- Keep velocity readable. The site can feel kinetic, but the professional message must stay legible within seconds.
- Connect the lab to the lead. Trevisoft, social channels, and audio should feel like credible extensions of the same maker.
- Prefer memorable specificity over portfolio convention. A smaller number of distinctive details beats a broad catalog of generic claims.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG AA contrast and keyboard-accessible interactions. Motion and audio must respect user control, reduced-motion preferences, and browser autoplay constraints. Social links need durable accessible names, focus states must remain visible, and responsive layouts should preserve the first-read hierarchy on small screens.
